## Chapter 1: ল্যাবরেটরির নিরাপদ ব্যবহার (Safe Use of Laboratory)
### Weight: 5-10 marks | Priority: ⚪ LOW

### Key Topics
1. **Lab Safety Rules** — Proper handling of chemicals, fire safety, fume hood usage
2. **Hazard Symbols** — Flammable, Toxic, Corrosive, Oxidizing, Irritant symbols
3. **First Aid** — Treatment for acid burns (wash with water, apply NaHCO₃), alkali burns (wash, apply boric acid)
4. **Standard Solution Preparation** — Primary standard substances (Na₂CO₃, K₂Cr₂O₇, Oxalic acid)
5. **Solution Preparation Math** — Dilution formula: V₁S₁ = V₂S₂

### Must-Know for MCQ
- Primary standards must have: high purity, stability, known formula, high molar mass
- Secondary standards: NaOH, HCl, KMnO₄ (standardized against primary)
- Molarity (M) = moles of solute / volume of solution in Liters
- Normality (N) = equivalents of solute / volume in Liters
- Relationship: N = n × M (where n = basicity or acidity)

## Chapter 2: গুণগত রসায়ন (Qualitative Chemistry)
### Weight: 20-25 marks | Priority: 🟡 HIGH

### Section A: Atomic Structure

#### Bohr's Atomic Model
- Electrons revolve in fixed circular orbits (energy levels: n = 1, 2, 3...)
- Energy of orbit: $E_n = -\frac{13.6}{n^2}$ eV (for hydrogen)
- Radius: $r_n = 0.529 \times n^2$ Å
- Energy is absorbed when electron jumps UP, emitted when it falls DOWN

#### Hydrogen Spectrum Series
| Series | Transition | Region | Formula |
|--------|-----------|--------|---------|
| Lyman | n → 1 | UV | 1/λ = R(1/1² - 1/n²) |
| Balmer | n → 2 | Visible | 1/λ = R(1/2² - 1/n²) |
| Paschen | n → 3 | IR | 1/λ = R(1/3² - 1/n²) |
| Brackett | n → 4 | IR | 1/λ = R(1/4² - 1/n²) |
| Pfund | n → 5 | Far IR | 1/λ = R(1/5² - 1/n²) |

**Rydberg Constant:** R = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹

#### Quantum Numbers
| Quantum Number | Symbol | Values | Meaning |
|---------------|--------|--------|---------|
| Principal | n | 1, 2, 3, 4... | Energy level/shell |
| Azimuthal | l | 0 to (n-1) | Subshell shape (0=s, 1=p, 2=d, 3=f) |
| Magnetic | m | -l to +l | Orbital orientation |
| Spin | s | +½ or -½ | Electron spin direction |

#### Electronic Configuration Rules
1. **Aufbau Principle:** Fill lowest energy orbitals first (1s→2s→2p→3s→3p→4s→3d→4p...)
2. **Pauli Exclusion:** Max 2 electrons per orbital, with opposite spins
3. **Hund's Rule:** Electrons fill degenerate orbitals singly before pairing

**⚠️ EXCEPTIONS (Board Exam Favorite!):**
- **Chromium (Cr, Z=24):** Expected: [Ar]3d⁴4s² → Actual: [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ (half-filled d stability)
- **Copper (Cu, Z=29):** Expected: [Ar]3d⁹4s² → Actual: [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹ (fully-filled d stability)

### Section B: Concentration & Solutions

#### Concentration Units
| Unit | Formula | Used For |
|------|---------|----------|
| Molarity (M) | mol/L | Most common |
| Molality (m) | mol/kg solvent | Temp-independent |
| Normality (N) | equivalents/L | Titrations |
| ppm | mg/L or mg/kg | Trace amounts |
| Mole Fraction (χ) | n₁/(n₁+n₂) | Gas mixtures |

### Section C: Solubility Product

#### Ksp and Kip
- **Ksp (Solubility Product):** Product of ion concentrations at saturation equilibrium
- **Kip (Ionic Product):** Product of actual ion concentrations
- If **Kip > Ksp** → Precipitation occurs ✅
- If **Kip < Ksp** → Solution is unsaturated, no precipitation ❌
- If **Kip = Ksp** → Saturated solution

**Example:** For AgCl: AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)
$$K_{sp} = [Ag^+][Cl^-]$$

### Section D: Chromatography
- **Paper Chromatography:** Separating mixtures by differential migration
- **Rf value** = Distance traveled by solute / Distance traveled by solvent front
- Rf is always between 0 and 1
- Each compound has a unique Rf under same conditions

## Chapter 3: মৌলের পর্যায়বৃত্ত ধর্ম ও রাসায়নিক বন্ধন (Periodic Properties & Chemical Bonding)
### Weight: 20-25 marks | Priority: 🟠 VERY HIGH

### Section A: Periodic Trends

| Property | Along Period (→) | Down Group (↓) |
|----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Atomic Radius | Decreases ↓ | Increases ↑ |
| Ionic Radius | Decreases (for cations) | Increases |
| Ionization Energy | Increases ↑ | Decreases ↓ |
| Electron Affinity | Generally increases | Decreases |
| Electronegativity | Increases ↑ | Decreases ↓ |
| Metallic Character | Decreases ↓ | Increases ↑ |

**⚠️ EXCEPTIONS (MCQ Traps!):**
- **IE:** N (2s²2p³) > O (2s²2p⁴) because half-filled 2p is extra stable
- **EA:** Cl > F because F is too small (high e⁻-e⁻ repulsion)
- **IE:** Be > B because 2s² is more stable than 2s²2p¹
- **EA of Noble gases** = 0 (full octet)

### Section B: Chemical Bonding

#### Hybridization Master Table
| Hybridization | Bond Angle | Shape | Example |
|--------------|------------|-------|---------|
| sp | 180° | Linear | BeCl₂, C₂H₂ |
| sp² | 120° | Trigonal Planar | BF₃, C₂H₄ |
| sp³ | 109.5° | Tetrahedral | CH₄, CCl₄ |
| sp³ (2 lp) | 104.5° | Bent/V-shape | H₂O |
| sp³ (1 lp) | 107° | Pyramidal | NH₃ |
| sp³d | 90°, 120° | Trigonal Bipyramidal | PCl₅ |
| sp³d² | 90° | Octahedral | SF₆ |

#### Fajan's Rules (Covalent Character in Ionic Bonds)
Covalent character INCREASES with:
1. **Small cation** (high charge density)
2. **Large anion** (easily polarized)
3. **High charge on cation**
4. **Pseudo-noble gas configuration** (d electrons don't shield well)

**Application:** CuCl is more covalent than NaCl (Cu⁺ has 3d¹⁰, doesn't shield well)

#### Hydrogen Bonding
- **Intermolecular:** Between different molecules (H₂O, HF, NH₃)
  - Causes higher boiling points
- **Intramolecular:** Within same molecule (o-nitrophenol)
  - Causes LOWER boiling point than expected

#### Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT)
- **Bond Order** = (Bonding electrons - Antibonding electrons) / 2
- If Bond Order > 0 → molecule is stable
- **O₂:** Bond order = 2, **paramagnetic** (unpaired e⁻ in π*)
- **N₂:** Bond order = 3, **diamagnetic** (all paired)
- **Bond Order ∝ Bond Strength ∝ 1/Bond Length**

## Chapter 4: রাসায়নিক পরিবর্তন (Chemical Change)
### Weight: 25-30 marks | Priority: 🔴 HIGHEST

### Section A: Chemical Equilibrium

#### Law of Mass Action
For: aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD
$$K_c = \frac{[C]^c[D]^d}{[A]^a[B]^b}$$

#### Kp and Kc Relationship
$$K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$$
Where Δn = (c+d) - (a+b) for gaseous species only
- R = 0.0821 L·atm·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹
- T in Kelvin

**When Δn = 0:** Kp = Kc (dimensionless)

#### Le Chatelier's Principle
| Change | Equilibrium Shifts | Effect on Product |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| ↑ Concentration of reactant | → Forward | More product |
| ↑ Concentration of product | ← Backward | Less product |
| ↑ Pressure (Δn > 0) | → Side with fewer moles | Depends |
| ↑ Temperature (exothermic) | ← Backward | Less product |
| ↑ Temperature (endothermic) | → Forward | More product |
| Catalyst added | No shift | No change in K |

**Industrial Application — Haber Process:**
N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ (ΔH = -92 kJ/mol, exothermic)
- **Conditions:** 200 atm, 450°C, Fe catalyst
- High pressure favors forward (fewer moles)
- Moderate temp (compromise: speed vs. yield)

### Section B: Acids & Bases

#### pH Calculations
| Type | Formula |
|------|---------|
| Strong acid | pH = -log[H⁺] |
| Strong base | pOH = -log[OH⁻], pH = 14 - pOH |
| Weak acid | pH = ½(pKa - log c) |
| Weak base | pOH = ½(pKb - log c), pH = 14 - pOH |

#### Buffer Solutions
**Acidic Buffer** (Weak acid + Conjugate base salt):
$$pH = pK_a + \log\frac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Acid}]}$$

**Basic Buffer** (Weak base + Conjugate acid salt):
$$pOH = pK_b + \log\frac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Base}]}$$

### Section C: Thermochemistry
- **Hess's Law:** ΔH of reaction = sum of ΔH of steps
- **Enthalpy of formation, combustion, neutralization**
- **Entropy (ΔS):** Disorder increases → ΔS > 0
- **Gibbs Free Energy:** ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
  - ΔG < 0 → Spontaneous
  - ΔG > 0 → Non-spontaneous
  - ΔG = 0 → Equilibrium

## Chapter 1: পরিবেশ রসায়ন (Environmental Chemistry)
### Weight: 10-15 marks | Priority: 🟢 MEDIUM

### Section A: Gas Laws

#### The Big 5 Gas Laws
| Law | Formula | Constant |
|-----|---------|----------|
| Boyle's Law | P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ | T, n |
| Charles's Law | V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ | P, n |
| Gay-Lussac's | P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂ | V, n |
| Avogadro's | V₁/n₁ = V₂/n₂ | T, P |
| **Ideal Gas** | **PV = nRT** | — |

**R values:** 
- 0.0821 L·atm·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹
- 8.314 J·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹

#### Dalton's Law of Partial Pressure
$$P_{total} = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + ...$$
$$P_i = \chi_i \times P_{total}$$

#### Graham's Law of Diffusion
$$\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \sqrt{\frac{M_2}{M_1}}$$

### Section B: Environmental Topics

#### Greenhouse Effect & Global Warming
- Greenhouse gases: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs, O₃, H₂O vapor
- CO₂ traps IR radiation → Earth temperature rises
- **Consequences:** Sea level rise, weather changes, glacier melting

#### Ozone Layer Depletion
- CFC + UV → Cl• (free radical)
- Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂
- ClO• + O → Cl• + O₂ (Cl• regenerated — chain reaction!)
- **Montreal Protocol** — International agreement to ban CFCs

#### Water Quality Parameters
| Parameter | Full Form | Meaning |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| BOD | Biochemical Oxygen Demand | O₂ needed by microbes to decompose organic matter. High BOD = polluted |
| COD | Chemical Oxygen Demand | O₂ needed to chemically oxidize organic + inorganic matter |
| TDS | Total Dissolved Solids | Total dissolved substances. Safe: <500 ppm |
| DO | Dissolved Oxygen | O₂ dissolved in water. High DO = clean water |

#### Air Pollutants
| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| CO | Incomplete combustion | Binds hemoglobin (COHb), reduces O₂ transport |
| SO₂ | Burning sulfur-containing fuels | Acid rain (H₂SO₄) |
| NOₓ | Vehicle exhaust, lightning | Smog, acid rain (HNO₃) |
| SPM | Dust, soot | Respiratory diseases |

## Chapter 2: জৈব রসায়ন (Organic Chemistry)
### Weight: 25-30 marks | Priority: 🔴 HIGHEST — THE GAME CHANGER

### Section A: Fundamentals

#### IUPAC Naming Rules
1. Find the **longest carbon chain** containing the functional group
2. Number from the end **nearest to the functional group**
3. Name substituents with position numbers
4. Priority order: -COOH > -CHO > -CO- > -OH > -NH₂ > C=C > C≡C

#### Types of Isomerism
```
Isomerism
├── Structural Isomerism
│   ├── Chain Isomerism (different carbon skeleton)
│   ├── Position Isomerism (different position of group)
│   └── Functional Group Isomerism (different functional group)
└── Stereoisomerism
    ├── Geometrical/cis-trans (restricted rotation)
    └── Optical (chiral center, mirror images)
```

#### Reaction Types
| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Substitution | One atom/group replaced | CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl |
| Addition | Atoms added across double/triple bond | C₂H₄ + HBr → C₂H₅Br |
| Elimination | Atoms removed to form double bond | C₂H₅OH → C₂H₄ + H₂O |

#### Reactive Intermediates
| Intermediate | Structure | Stability Order |
|-------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Carbocation (R⁺) | C with 6 e⁻ | 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃⁺ |
| Carbanion (R⁻) | C with 8 e⁻ | CH₃⁻ > 1° > 2° > 3° |
| Free Radical (R•) | C with 7 e⁻ | 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃• |

### Section B: Hydrocarbons

#### Alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂)
- **Preparation:** Wurtz reaction: 2R-X + 2Na → R-R + 2NaX
- **Reactions:** Halogenation (free radical substitution)
  - CH₄ + Cl₂ →(UV) CH₃Cl + HCl

#### Alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ)
- **Preparation:** Dehydration of alcohol: R-OH →(H₂SO₄, 170°C) Alkene + H₂O
- **Key Reactions:**
  - **Markovnikov's Rule:** H adds to C with MORE H's
    - CH₃CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CHBrCH₃ (major product)
  - **Anti-Markovnikov (Peroxide effect):** With benzoyl peroxide
    - CH₃CH=CH₂ + HBr →(peroxide) CH₃CH₂CH₂Br
  - **Ozonolysis:** Alkene + O₃ → Ozonide →(Zn/H₂O) 2 Aldehydes/Ketones
  - **Polymerization:** nCH₂=CH₂ →(catalyst) (-CH₂-CH₂-)ₙ (polyethylene)

#### Alkynes (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂)
- **Preparation:** CaC₂ + 2H₂O → C₂H₂ + Ca(OH)₂
- **Key Reactions:**
  - Terminal alkynes + NaNH₂ → Sodium acetylide (acidic hydrogen!)
  - Hydration: C₂H₂ + H₂O →(HgSO₄, H₂SO₄) CH₃CHO

### Section C: Aromatic Compounds

#### Benzene Structure
- 6 C atoms in hexagonal ring, all sp² hybridized
- Delocalized π electrons above and below ring plane
- Resonance energy = 150 kJ/mol
- Undergoes **electrophilic substitution** (not addition — preserves aromaticity)

#### Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution Reactions
| Reaction | Reagent | Product |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Halogenation | Cl₂/AlCl₃ (or Br₂/FeBr₃) | Chlorobenzene |
| Nitration | Conc. HNO₃ + Conc. H₂SO₄ | Nitrobenzene |
| Sulfonation | Fuming H₂SO₄ (SO₃ + H₂SO₄) | Benzenesulfonic acid |
| Friedel-Crafts Alkylation | R-Cl + AlCl₃ | Toluene (with CH₃Cl) |
| Friedel-Crafts Acylation | RCOCl + AlCl₃ | Acetophenone (with CH₃COCl) |

#### Directing Groups (BOARD EXAM ESSENTIAL!)
| Type | Groups | Direct To |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Ortho/Para Directing** (Activating) | -OH, -NH₂, -OR, -CH₃, -NHCOCH₃ | o/p positions |
| **Ortho/Para Directing** (Deactivating) | -Cl, -Br, -I | o/p (but deactivate ring) |
| **Meta Directing** (Deactivating) | -NO₂, -COOH, -CHO, -COR, -CN, -SO₃H | meta position |

**Rule of thumb:** 
- Groups that DONATE electrons → Activating → o/p
- Groups that WITHDRAW electrons → Deactivating → meta
- Halogens are exceptions (withdraw by -I but donate by +M → o/p but deactivating)

### Section D: Functional Group Chemistry

#### Alcohols
| Classification | Structure | Lucas Test |
|---------------|-----------|------------|
| Primary (1°) | R-CH₂OH | No reaction at room temp |
| Secondary (2°) | R₂CHOH | Turbid in 5 minutes |
| Tertiary (3°) | R₃COH | Turbid immediately |

#### Aldehydes vs Ketones — Distinction Tests
| Test | Aldehyde | Ketone |
|------|----------|--------|
| Tollens' Test (AgNO₃/NH₃) | Silver mirror ✅ | No reaction ❌ |
| Fehling's Test (CuSO₄/NaOH) | Red precipitate (Cu₂O) ✅ | No reaction ❌ |
| 2,4-DNP Test | Orange/Yellow precipitate ✅ | Orange/Yellow precipitate ✅ |
| Iodoform Test | ✅ for CH₃CHO only | ✅ for CH₃COR |
| Schiff's Test | Pink color ✅ | No reaction ❌ |

#### Important Name Reactions
| Reaction | What Happens | Equation |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Wurtz** | 2 Alkyl halides + Na → Alkane | 2CH₃Cl + 2Na → C₂H₆ + 2NaCl |
| **Friedel-Crafts** | Benzene + R-Cl/RCOCl → substituted | C₆H₆ + CH₃Cl →(AlCl₃) C₆H₅CH₃ + HCl |
| **Aldol Condensation** | 2 Aldehydes → β-hydroxy aldehyde | 2CH₃CHO →(NaOH) CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO |
| **Cannizzaro** | Aldehyde (no α-H) + NaOH → acid + alcohol | 2HCHO + NaOH → HCOONa + CH₃OH |
| **Kolbe** | Na salt of acid → Alkane | 2CH₃COONa →(electrolysis) C₂H₆ |
| **Reimer-Tiemann** | Phenol + CHCl₃/NaOH → Salicylaldehyde | C₆H₅OH + CHCl₃ + NaOH → o-HOC₆H₄CHO |

### Weight: 15-20 marks | Priority: 🟡 HIGH

### Titration Mathematics

#### Acid-Base Titration
$$n_a V_a S_a = n_b V_b S_b$$
Where n = basicity/acidity

#### Key Indicators
| Indicator | pH Range | Color Change | Best For |
|-----------|----------|-------------|----------|
| Methyl Orange | 3.1 - 4.4 | Red → Yellow | Strong acid + Strong/Weak base |
| Phenolphthalein | 8.3 - 10.0 | Colorless → Pink | Strong base + Strong/Weak acid |
| Methyl Red | 4.4 - 6.2 | Red → Yellow | — |

#### Redox Titration
- **Oxidation:** Loss of electrons (increase in oxidation number)
- **Reduction:** Gain of electrons (decrease in oxidation number)
- **Ion-electron method** for balancing redox equations

### Stoichiometry
- **Limiting reagent:** The reactant that runs out first
- **Percentage yield** = (Actual yield / Theoretical yield) × 100%
- **Percentage purity** = (Mass of pure substance / Total mass) × 100%

## Chapter 4: তড়িৎ রসায়ন (Electrochemistry)
### Weight: 15-20 marks | Priority: 🟠 VERY HIGH

### Electrochemical Cells

#### Galvanic (Voltaic) Cell
- Converts chemical energy → electrical energy
- **Anode (-):** Oxidation occurs (Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻)
- **Cathode (+):** Reduction occurs (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu)
- **Salt bridge:** Maintains electrical neutrality

#### Daniel Cell Notation
$$Zn(s) | Zn^{2+}(aq) || Cu^{2+}(aq) | Cu(s)$$

#### Standard Electrode Potential
- Measured against **Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)** = 0.00V
- More positive E° = stronger oxidizing agent (wants to be reduced)
- More negative E° = stronger reducing agent (wants to be oxidized)

$$E°_{cell} = E°_{cathode} - E°_{anode}$$

### Nernst Equation
$$E_{cell} = E°_{cell} - \frac{0.0591}{n}\log Q$$
at 25°C (298 K)

Where Q = reaction quotient (same form as Kc but with actual concentrations)

### Faraday's Laws

#### First Law
$$W = \frac{MIt}{nF}$$
Where:
- W = mass deposited (g)
- M = molar mass (g/mol)
- I = current (A)
- t = time (s)
- n = electrons transferred
- F = 96500 C/mol

#### Second Law
When same charge passes through different electrolytes in series:
$$\frac{W_1}{W_2} = \frac{E_1}{E_2} = \frac{M_1/n_1}{M_2/n_2}$$

### Common Cells
| Cell | Type | Voltage | Features |
|------|------|---------|----------|
| Dry Cell (Leclanché) | Primary | 1.5V | Zn anode, MnO₂ cathode, NH₄Cl paste |
| Lead-Acid Battery | Secondary | 2V/cell | Pb anode, PbO₂ cathode, H₂SO₄ |
| Lithium-Ion | Secondary | 3.6V | Rechargeable, high energy density |
| Fuel Cell (H₂-O₂) | — | — | 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, clean energy |
